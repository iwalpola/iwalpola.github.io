In the classroom

Most of science happens where nobody can look.

Pupils are asked to believe in particles they cannot see, currents they cannot follow and signals racing down nerves faster than they can blink. A diagram asks them to take it on trust. A simulation lets them watch it happen.

So I build the simulations I wish I had on the board when explaining an idea for the third time. The physics on screen is calculated, not drawn to look about right. Each is a single HTML file you can open from a memory stick and show on a projector.

Physics · Particles Brownian motion Grains jiggle because molecules too small to see keep hitting them. Physics · States of matter Melting snowman Watch a crystal come apart while the heating curve goes flat. Physics · Electricity Conduction and resistance Why a thin wire resists more than a thick one, one electron at a time. Biology · Nervous system The reflex arc A story of a signal, told over a real model of the human body.

The long project

Fixing tragedies of the commons with a network.

When every actor does the sensible thing and the result is ruinous for all of them, that is a coordination failure. The pattern is sometimes called Moloch. Overfished seas, burnt-out commons and races to the bottom all follow it.

I have spent more than a decade researching this. Many communities are already building the alternatives, such as co-ops, land trusts, mutual aid and platform cooperatives. What they lack is any way of finding each other. The knowledge exists and the people exist, but each sits in its own silo.

The Coordination Layer is my answer. It is a shared, inspectable knowledge graph of people, projects and ideas, with an AI interface that sits on top of the platforms communities already use. Nobody has to migrate.

Concept v0.2 · early versions being built

Read the concept document on GitHub →

  • The graph is the memory.The AI has no hidden state. Everything it knows is a sourced entry you can inspect and delete.
  • The index may suggest; only the graph may assert.A similarity match is a proposal. It becomes a fact only once confirmed.
  • Dissent is data.Where communities genuinely disagree, the layer records the divergence rather than smoothing it into false consensus.

Both are the same idea. Most of what matters is a system you can't see until somebody draws it.
